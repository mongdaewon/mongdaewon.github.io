There are two ways to combine audio files on an iPhone, and they are not the same job.

- **Back to back:** one file plays, then the next. A recording that stopped halfway, a podcast with an intro and an outro, a few voice memos you want as one file.
- **Layered:** two sounds play at the same time. A voice over background music, a sound effect under narration, two takes mixed together.

We make an app for each: [JoinSound](/apps/joinsound/) for back to back, and [AudioJoin](/apps/audiojoin/) for layering. Below is the same three recordings opened in both, so you can see the difference.

## How to combine audio files on iPhone, one after another

If all you want is one file out of several, a list is the fastest way there. There is nothing to place on a timeline: the order of the list is the order of the file.

1. Open JoinSound and pick your recordings, or share them in from another app.
2. Drag the rows into order. Tap the scissors on a row to trim its start or end on the waveform.
3. Tap **Preview Result** to hear the whole thing, with a yellow mark wherever one file meets the next.
4. Pick M4A, MP3 or WAV, and tap **Merge into one**.

A few switches cover what usually goes wrong when recordings are joined:

- **Match loudness** evens out a quiet file next to a loud one.
- **Smooth joins** adds a short fade wherever one file meets the next, so the cut doesn't click.
- **Shorten silences**, inside each file's trim screen, takes out long pauses. *Natural* leaves short pauses alone, *Tight* cuts closer.

When your files share the same format and nothing needs changing, M4A is saved without re-encoding, so the audio keeps its original quality.

## How to layer audio on iPhone

Layering needs a timeline, because you have to say *when* each sound starts. That is what AudioJoin is for.

1. Open AudioJoin and add your recordings. Each one becomes a block on a track.
2. Put the voice on one track and the music on another. Blocks that line up vertically play at the same time.
3. Drag a block to slide it in time, and pull its edges to trim it. Its own volume slider lets the music sit under the voice instead of over it.
4. Save as one file in M4A, MP3 or WAV.

Because every clip is its own block, you can also do things a list can't: change one clip's playback speed (0.5x to 2x, without changing the pitch), split a clip in two, duplicate it, or add a gap of silence before or after it.

AudioJoin can join files back to back too. Just put them on one track. But if that is all you need, the extra controls are in the way, and JoinSound gets you there in fewer taps.

## Which one should you use?

| You want to... | Use |
|---|---|
| Turn several recordings into one file | JoinSound |
| Put an intro and an outro around a recording | JoinSound |
| Fix one quiet file among loud ones | JoinSound (Match loudness) |
| Put a voice over background music | AudioJoin |
| Make one clip quieter or slower than the rest | AudioJoin |
| Start a sound partway through another | AudioJoin |

A simple test: if you could describe the result as "this, then this, then this", use JoinSound. If you need the word "while", as in "the music plays while I talk", use AudioJoin.
